Allama Shibli Nomani (1857–1914) was one of the most influential scholars, poets, and historians of modern South Asia. Born in **Bindwal, Azamgarh**, in the heart of Uttar Pradesh, he devoted his life to reviving learning and bridging classical knowledge with the modern world.

## Early life and education

Shibli was born into a respected family and received a traditional education in Arabic, Persian, and Islamic sciences. A voracious reader from a young age, he mastered several languages and immersed himself in history, philosophy, and literature.

## A scholar and reformer

Shibli believed that education was the key to the progress of his community. He taught at the **Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental College** (later Aligarh Muslim University) alongside Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, and travelled across the Ottoman Empire, Egypt, and Syria to study manuscripts and meet leading intellectuals.

His major contributions include:

- Founding the **Nadwatul Ulama** institution in Lucknow.
- Establishing the **Darul Musannifin (Shibli Academy)** in Azamgarh — a research institute that still functions today.
- Pioneering modern historical writing in Urdu.

## Literary legacy

Shibli was a gifted poet and a trailblazing biographer. His celebrated works include *Al-Farooq*, *Seerat-un-Nabi*, and *Sher-ul-Ajam* (a history of Persian poetry). His prose set new standards for Urdu scholarship and is studied to this day.
