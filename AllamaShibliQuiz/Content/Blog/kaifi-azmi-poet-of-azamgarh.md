Azamgarh has given India many remarkable minds, but few shine as brightly as **Kaifi Azmi (1919–2002)** — poet, lyricist, and lifelong champion of the underprivileged.

## Rooted in Azamgarh

Born as Athar Husain Rizvi in the village of **Mijwan, Azamgarh**, Kaifi began writing poetry as a teenager. His early verses already carried the themes that would define his life: justice, equality, and compassion for the poor.

## Poetry with a purpose

Kaifi belonged to the **Progressive Writers' Movement**, using his pen to question injustice and inspire hope. His poems were not written for the drawing room — they were written for the factory worker, the farmer, and the woman denied her rights.

Some of his most admired collections include *Jhankar*, *Aakhir-e-Shab*, and *Awara Sajde*, the last of which won him the Sahitya Akademi Award.

## A voice in Indian cinema

Kaifi carried his poetry to the silver screen, writing unforgettable lyrics and dialogue for films such as *Kaagaz Ke Phool*, *Haqeeqat*, and *Garm Hava*. He proved that popular art and social conscience could live together.

## Giving back to Mijwan

Perhaps his greatest legacy is **Mijwan** itself. Kaifi transformed his birthplace with schools, a girls' college, roads, and a women's empowerment centre — showing that a true son of the soil never forgets where he came from.

## The lesson for young learners

Kaifi Azmi's story reminds students that talent rooted in values can change not just one life, but an entire community. Azamgarh's greatest personalities were, first and foremost, lifelong learners — just like the students taking part in the Brainiac Battle today.
