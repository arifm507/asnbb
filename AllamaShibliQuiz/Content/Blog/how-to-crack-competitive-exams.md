Every competitive exam rewards the same thing: **consistent, smart preparation**. You do not need to be the most talented student in the room — you need a plan and the discipline to follow it. Here is how.

## 1. Understand the exam before you start

Before opening a single book, study the **syllabus and past papers**. Know the pattern, the marking scheme, and which topics carry the most weight. Preparation without direction wastes time.

## 2. Build a realistic study plan

- Break the syllabus into **weekly goals**, not one giant mountain.
- Study in focused blocks (try 45 minutes of work, 10 minutes of rest).
- Keep one day a week lighter — rest prevents burnout.

## 3. Master the basics first

Competitive exams test fundamentals under pressure. Make sure your **concepts are clear** before chasing advanced tricks. A strong foundation makes everything else easier.

## 4. Practise, then practise again

> You do not rise to the level of the exam; you fall to the level of your practice.

Solve **mock tests and previous years' papers** under timed conditions. This builds speed, exposes weak areas, and removes exam-day fear.

## 5. Revise smartly

- Keep a short **notebook of formulas and key facts**.
- Use **active recall** — close the book and test yourself.
- Revisit mistakes; your error log is your best teacher.

## 6. Look after yourself

Sleep, simple exercise, and good food matter more than one extra hour of late-night cramming. A rested mind remembers more.

## 7. On exam day

Stay calm, read every question carefully, attempt the easy ones first, and keep an eye on the clock. Confidence comes from preparation — and you have done the work.
